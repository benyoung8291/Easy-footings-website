How Much Do Screw Piles Cost in Victoria? 2026 Price Guide

A clear breakdown of screw pile pricing across Victoria — per pile, per project, and the hidden savings most builders don't consider.

Published 11 April 2026

Whether you're building a deck in Melbourne's outer suburbs, restumping a weatherboard in Ballarat, or laying the foundation for a granny flat on the Mornington Peninsula, one of the first questions you'll ask is: how much will the footings cost?

Understanding screw pile pricing helps you budget accurately, compare quotes with confidence, and avoid unexpected costs that often come with traditional concrete footings. This guide breaks down what Victorian builders and homeowners can expect to pay for screw pile footings in 2026.

If you're still weighing up your options, our screw piles vs concrete footings comparison is a helpful starting point.

Typical Screw Pile Pricing in Victoria

Screw pile costs in Victoria typically range between $80 and $200 per pile installed, depending on the pile diameter, required depth, soil conditions, and the product used. Most residential projects fall within this range.

Smaller-diameter piles used for lightweight structures like timber decks sit at the lower end of this range, while larger helical piles for heavy-duty applications such as granny flats or commercial builds will be closer to the upper end.

It's important to note that screw pile pricing is almost always quoted as a fully installed price — meaning the pile, the labour, and the equipment are all included. There's no separate bill for concrete, formwork, or excavation.

Factors That Affect Screw Pile Cost

No two sites are identical. Here are the main factors that influence what you'll pay:

Pile Diameter

Larger piles carry greater loads but cost more to manufacture and install. A 76mm pile for a deck costs less than a 114mm pile for a granny flat subfloor.

Depth Required

Piles are driven until they reach adequate bearing capacity. Deeper installations on softer soils or reactive clay sites may require extensions, which adds to the cost.

Soil Conditions

Rocky ground, heavy clay, or waterlogged sites can affect installation speed and equipment requirements. A straightforward sandy-loam site is typically the most cost-effective.

Site Access

Tight access, steep slopes, or inner-city properties may require compact machinery or additional setup time. Open, flat sites with good vehicle access are the most straightforward.

Number of Piles

Larger projects with more piles benefit from economies of scale. The per-pile cost for a 30-pile granny flat job will generally be lower than for a 6-pile deck.

Product Type

Different products suit different applications. RapidStump for decks and sheds, StumpRite for restumping, and SurePile for heavy-duty and commercial — each is priced to its purpose.

Hidden Savings Compared to Concrete Footings

When comparing screw piles to concrete, the per-footing price only tells half the story. The real savings are in what you don't have to pay for:

No Excavation Costs

Concrete footings require holes to be dug, spoil to be removed, and the site to be cleaned up. Screw piles go straight into the ground with minimal disturbance.

No Concrete Trucks or Pump Hire

Concrete delivery, pump hire, and formwork materials all add to the cost of traditional footings — none of which are needed with screw piles.

No Curing Time — Faster Trades on Site

Concrete needs days to cure before you can build on it. Screw piles are load-bearing immediately, so your carpenter, steel worker, or shed installer can start the same day. That's real money saved in reduced project timelines.

No Weather Delays

You can't pour concrete in the rain, and Victorian weather is unpredictable. Screw piles can be installed in almost any weather conditions, keeping your build on schedule.

Less Site Remediation

Concrete pours leave mess — spills, washout, formwork waste. Screw piles leave your site clean and ready to go, with no skip bins or tip runs needed.
