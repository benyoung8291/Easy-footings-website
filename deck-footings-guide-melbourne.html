The Complete Guide to Deck Footings in Melbourne

From footing depth requirements to the best systems for your deck build, this guide covers everything Melbourne homeowners and builders need to know.

Decks are one of Melbourne's most popular home improvements. Whether you're building an alfresco entertaining area, a pool surround, or a simple garden deck, getting the footings right is crucial. The footings carry the entire load of your deck and everything on it — they need to be properly sized, correctly spaced, and suited to your soil conditions.

This guide covers the main deck footing options available in Melbourne, what determines footing depth, how many piles you'll need, and why screw piles have become the preferred choice for most deck builds.

Deck Footing Requirements in Melbourne

Deck footing depth in Melbourne depends on your soil classification under AS 2870. On stable (Class A or S) sites, footings may only need to reach 300–450mm. On reactive clay sites (Class M, H1, H2), footings need to go deeper — often 600mm to 1,500mm or more — to avoid movement from seasonal soil expansion and contraction.

Your deck design also matters. Elevated decks, decks attached to the house, and decks over a certain size all have specific engineering requirements under the National Construction Code. A building surveyor or structural engineer can confirm exact requirements for your project.

Types of Deck Footings

Concrete Piers / Pads

The traditional approach. Holes are dug, formwork is placed, and concrete is poured with steel reinforcement. Requires excavation, concrete delivery, and 7–28 days of curing before you can build. Weather dependent — rain can delay the pour and affect quality.

Screw Piles

Steel piles with helical plates are mechanically screwed into the ground. No excavation, no concrete, no curing time. Install in hours and build your deck the same day. Work in all weather and all soil types including Melbourne's reactive clay. The modern standard for deck footings.

Pre-Cast Piers

Pre-manufactured concrete blocks placed on compacted ground. Only suitable for very small, low decks on stable soil. Limited load capacity and not suitable for reactive clay sites. Not acceptable for most building permit applications.

Do I Need a Permit for My Deck?

In Victoria, some small, low decks may be exempt from building permits, but most decks — particularly those attached to the house, elevated more than 800mm, or over a certain area — require a building permit. The permit covers the entire structure, including footings.

Screw piles simplify the approval process because they come with engineering certifications that building surveyors accept. For a full overview, see our council approval guide.
