What Are Adjustable Stumps?

Adjustable stumps are engineered steel house stumps fitted with a threaded top assembly that allows the height of each stump to be fine-tuned after it is installed. Instead of packing bearers with shims or accepting whatever level a fixed concrete stump gives you, an adjustable stump lets the installer wind the head up or down until every bearer sits perfectly level.

Our StumpRite adjustable stump system takes this a step further: the stumps are driven or screwed into the ground mechanically, with no excavation and no concrete. That means no curing time, no weather delays and no concrete trucks — you can start building the same day your adjustable footings go in.

Because the adjustment is built into the stump head, re-levelling years later takes minutes with hand tools. In Victoria's reactive clay soils, where seasonal movement is the leading cause of sloping floors and cracked plaster, that adjustability is the difference between a quick tune-up and a major restumping job. How We Install Adjustable Stumps

This is the licensed install path. It is not a product catalogue. StumpRite SKUs stay on the product page.

1. Inspect

Under-floor assessment: failed stumps, access, soil, and whether the job is a full or partial restump, a deck, or a new stump line.

2. Engineer

Footing design from the soil class and loads. RapidStump, StumpRite or SurePile is specified to the site, not picked from a catalogue.

3. Permit

House restumping is structural work in Victoria and generally needs a building permit. See the restumping permit guide.

4. Install

Steel stumps are driven or screwed without concrete. The house or frame is jacked, old stumps come out, new ones go in. No 7–28 day cure.

5. Adjust

Threaded heads are wound to a dead-level bearer line on the day. Later clay movement is corrected from the top with hand tools, without excavation.

Reactive Clay, Sloping Blocks and Timeframe

Much of Melbourne’s west and north, and large parts of regional Victoria, sit on reactive clay. Fixed concrete stumps heave and settle with that moisture cycle. Adjustable steel is driven or screwed below the reactive surface layer, then re-levelled from the top. That is the install reason we specify adjustable heads on clay, not a catalogue extra.

On sloping blocks every stump needs a different height. Cutting and packing fixed posts is slow and hard to keep level. Adjustable heads make the bearer line straightforward on grades. See the sloping block footing guide.

Typical install timeframe for concrete-free restumping with driven steel is 1–3 days, with the house lowered the same day. Traditional concrete restumping takes 1–2 weeks or more because of the 7–28 day cure. Access, stump count and engineering still move a programme. Full cost ranges stay at $500–$1,000+ per stump, or $20,000–$50,000 for a full restump, as set out in the restumping cost guide.

Adjustable Stumps vs Fixed Concrete Stumps

Much of Melbourne and regional Victoria sits on highly reactive clay. As the clay wets and dries through the seasons, it swells and shrinks — and fixed stumps move with it. With traditional concrete stumps, correcting that movement means jacking the house and digging out stumps. With adjustable stumps, it means turning a threaded head. For timber vs concrete vs steel as materials, see steel vs timber vs concrete stumps in Victoria.

Our adjustable footing systems are also installed differently: they are mechanically driven or screwed past the reactive surface layer into stable ground, rather than sitting in a hand-dug hole filled with concrete. The result is a footing that is both more stable from day one and far easier to maintain over the life of the building.

Every adjustable stump we install is engineer certified to Australian standards, with the system specified from your soil report and load requirements. Adjustable Stump FAQs

What are adjustable stumps?

Adjustable stumps are steel house stumps with a threaded head that lets you fine-tune the height of each stump after installation. They are used to achieve a perfectly level floor on new builds, decks and extensions, and to re-level existing homes during restumping. Systems like StumpRite are installed without concrete, so there is no curing time before you build.

How much height adjustment do adjustable stumps provide?

Most adjustable stump systems provide fine height adjustment via a threaded top assembly, typically allowing tens of millimetres of adjustment after installation. This is enough to level bearers precisely on installation day and to re-level the structure later if seasonal soil movement occurs.

Are adjustable stumps better than concrete stumps in Victoria's reactive clay?

In reactive clay soils common across Melbourne's west and north, fixed concrete stumps can heave and settle with seasonal moisture change, and re-levelling them means excavation. Adjustable steel stumps anchor below the reactive surface layer and can be re-levelled from the top with hand tools, making ongoing maintenance far simpler.

Do you install adjustable stumps across all of Victoria?

Yes. Easy Footings supplies and installs adjustable stumps and adjustable footings across Melbourne, Geelong, Ballarat, Bendigo, the Mornington Peninsula, Gippsland and regional Victoria.

Can adjustable stumps be used for restumping an existing house?

Yes. Adjustable steel stumps are ideal for restumping older Victorian homes. Failing timber or concrete stumps are replaced with engineer-certified adjustable stumps, the floor is re-levelled precisely, and future re-levelling can be done without excavation. See the restumping cost guide.

How long does adjustable stump installation take?

Concrete-free restumping with driven steel is typically completed in 1–3 days, with the house lowered the same day. Traditional concrete restumping takes 1–2 weeks or more because of the 7–28 day cure.

Do I need a licensed installer, or can I buy supply-only?

House restumping and anything needing a building permit or engineer certificate should be installed by a licensed professional. Supply-only StumpRite components can suit some simple Class 10 jobs with engineer drawings. See DIY vs professional installation.
