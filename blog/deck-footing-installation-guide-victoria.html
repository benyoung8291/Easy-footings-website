Deck Footing Installation Guide for Victorian Builders

The definitive guide to deck footings in Victoria — compare options, understand regulations, and choose the right system for your project.

Published 15 February 2026

Building a deck is one of the most popular home improvement projects in Victoria. Whether you're adding an outdoor entertaining area in Melbourne's inner suburbs or building a wraparound deck on a Mornington Peninsula holiday home, getting the footings right is critical to a safe, long-lasting result.

This guide covers everything you need to know about deck footings in Victoria: the different types available, why concrete-free options are surging in popularity, planning and permit requirements, soil considerations, costs, and common mistakes to avoid.

Types of Deck Footings

Victorian builders have several options for deck footings:

Traditional Concrete Piers

The conventional approach: dig a hole, place a cardboard tube or formwork, fill with concrete, embed a stirrup or post bracket, and wait 7–28 days for curing. Reliable but slow, weather-dependent, and labour-intensive.

Concrete-Free Screw Piles

Screw pile footings are mechanically driven into the ground using a hydraulic drive head. Systems like BMSA's SurePile provide immediate load capacity with zero concrete and zero curing time.

Concrete-Free Driven Stumps

Systems like RapidStump are driven or screwed into position and locked at the correct height. They're ideal for standard deck applications where speed and adjustability are priorities. See our product comparison for details.

Why Concrete-Free Deck Footings Are Gaining Popularity

Concrete-free deck footings have become the preferred choice for Victorian deck builders, and for good reason:

  • Same-day building — install footings in the morning, start framing in the afternoon
  • All-weather installation — no rain delays or cold-weather curing concerns
  • Adjustable height — fine-tune each footing for perfect deck level, especially on sloping sites
  • Minimal garden disruption — no excavation piles, no concrete splatter, no truck ruts
  • Eco-friendly — zero concrete means zero carbon emissions from cement
  • Removable — if the deck is ever demolished, footings can be unscrewed leaving minimal trace

Planning Your Deck Footings

Proper planning ensures your deck footings meet structural requirements and comply with Victorian regulations:

  • Load calculations — consider dead loads (deck weight), live loads (people, furniture), and any additional loads (spa, planter boxes)
  • Bearer and joist spans — these determine footing spacing. Typical residential deck footings are spaced 1.2m to 2.4m apart
  • Soil assessment — a soil report identifies the classification (A, S, M, H1, H2, E, P) and informs the footing specification
  • Site survey — check for underground services, easements, and setback requirements

Deck Footing Installation Process

For concrete-free deck footings, the process is efficient and straightforward:

  1. Set out — mark footing positions from the deck plan
  2. Install — hydraulic equipment drives each footing to the specified depth. Torque monitoring confirms capacity.
  3. Level — each footing is cut or adjusted to the correct height for the bearer line
  4. Bracket — post brackets or bearer saddles are fitted
  5. Build — bearers, joists, and decking go on immediately. No waiting.

For a typical backyard deck (10–15 footings), the entire footing installation is usually completed in 2–4 hours.

Deck Footing Regulations in Victoria

Key regulatory considerations for deck footings in Victoria:

  • Building permit — required for decks more than 800mm above ground level, or attached to the house
  • Structural plans — must be prepared by a designer or engineer showing footing locations, sizes, and depths
  • AS 1684 — the Australian Standard for residential timber framing, which governs deck construction
  • Setbacks — decks must comply with planning setbacks from boundaries
  • Bushfire zones — properties in designated BAL areas have additional construction requirements

Deck Footings for Different Soil Types

Victoria's diverse soils mean deck footings aren't one-size-fits-all:

  • Reactive clay (western/northern Melbourne) — screw piles anchor below the reactive zone for stable support
  • Sandy soil (bayside, Surf Coast) — larger helical plates or deeper piles for adequate bearing
  • Sloping sites (hills, peninsula) — adjustable concrete-free footings handle varying heights without complex formwork
  • Fill sites (new estates) — deeper piles reach natural ground below compacted fill

Cost of Deck Footings in Melbourne

Deck footing costs depend on the system chosen, number of footings, soil conditions, and site access. Concrete-free footings offer significant total-cost advantages:

  • No concrete ordering, delivery, or pumping costs
  • Reduced labour — faster installation means fewer hours on-site
  • No weather-related rescheduling costs
  • Follow-on trades can start immediately, reducing overall project duration

For most residential deck projects, concrete-free footings deliver better overall value than traditional concrete piers when total project costs are considered.

Common Deck Footing Mistakes to Avoid

  • Under-engineering — don't guess footing sizes or spacing. Get an engineer's specification.
  • Skipping the soil test — soil conditions vary dramatically across Melbourne and regional Victoria
  • Ignoring drainage — poor drainage around footings can undermine foundations over time
  • Wrong spacing — footings too far apart leads to bouncy, unsafe decks
  • Shallow depth — footings must reach below the reactive soil zone and meet minimum depth requirements

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a permit for deck footings in Victoria?
In Victoria, a building permit is generally required for decks that are more than 800mm above ground level, or attached to the house. Decks under 800mm that are freestanding and not in a flood or bushfire zone may be exempt. Always check with your local council or building surveyor.
How many footings does a deck need?
The number depends on deck size, design loads, bearer spans, and joist spacing. A typical residential deck might need 6 to 20 footings. Your structural engineer or designer will specify the exact number and spacing.
What is the best footing for a deck on a sloping block?
Concrete-free screw piles and adjustable stumps are ideal for sloping blocks. Their adjustable height means each footing can accommodate different ground levels without complex formwork. RapidStump from BMSA is particularly well-suited to sloping Victorian blocks.
How long do deck footings last?
Galvanised steel concrete-free footings have a design life of 50+ years. They're adjustable post-installation, allowing correction if ground movement occurs over time.
