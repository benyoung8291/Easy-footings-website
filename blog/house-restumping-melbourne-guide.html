House Restumping Melbourne: When to Restump & Your Best Options

Is your Melbourne home showing signs of foundation problems? Learn when restumping is needed and why concrete-free is the modern choice.

Published 1 February 2026

Melbourne is home to thousands of older properties — from Victorian-era weatherboard cottages to post-war brick homes — many of which sit on timber or early concrete stumps that are now decades past their useful life. If your floors are bouncy, doors are sticking, or you've noticed cracks appearing in walls, your home may need restumping.

In this guide, we explain what restumping involves, how to tell if your home needs it, the different systems available, costs, permits, and why concrete-free restumping is rapidly becoming the preferred method for Melbourne homeowners.

What Is House Restumping?

Restumping (also called reblocking) is the process of replacing the stumps (posts) that support a raised-floor house. Most older Melbourne homes are built on short stumps that elevate the house above ground level, creating a subfloor space. Over time, these stumps deteriorate, shift, or sink, causing the house to become unlevel.

Restumping involves jacking up the house, removing the old stumps, installing new ones, and re-levelling the entire structure. It's one of the most important maintenance investments a Melbourne homeowner can make.

Signs Your Melbourne Home Needs Restumping

Watch for these warning signs:

  • Bouncy or springy floors — floors that flex when you walk on them indicate stumps have shifted or sunk
  • Cracks in walls or ceilings — especially diagonal cracks near doors and windows
  • Doors and windows sticking — frames become misaligned as the house moves
  • Visible stump damage — if you can access the subfloor, look for rotting timber, crumbling concrete, or stumps that are leaning
  • Uneven floors — place a ball on the floor; if it rolls consistently in one direction, the house may be out of level
  • Gaps between floor and skirting — movement in the structure causes visible gaps
  • Plumbing problems — shifting foundations can stress pipes, causing leaks or blockages

Types of Restumping Systems

Timber Stumps (Traditional)

The original method — hardwood posts set in the ground. Timber stumps are prone to rot, termite damage, and moisture degradation. Most timber stumps in Melbourne last 25–50 years before needing replacement.

Concrete Stumps

Precast or poured concrete stumps became popular mid-century. While more durable than timber, they can crack, shift in reactive clay, and require excavation and curing time for replacement.

Steel Screw Piles

Screw pile systems are mechanically driven to depth, providing immediate load capacity. They're excellent for restumping as they cause minimal vibration to the existing structure.

Why Concrete-Free Restumping Is the Modern Choice

Concrete-free restumping offers compelling advantages over traditional concrete replacement:

  • Speed — a full restump can be completed in 1–3 days vs 1–2 weeks for concrete
  • No curing delays — the house can be re-levelled and lived in immediately
  • Adjustable — if the house settles further, concrete-free stumps can be re-adjusted without replacement
  • Minimal disruption — no concrete mixing in your yard, no heavy trucks, no wet trades
  • Works in all weather — no rain delays mean your project stays on schedule
  • Tight access friendly — systems designed for low-clearance subfloor spaces common in older Melbourne homes

The Restumping Process: Step by Step

  1. Assessment — a professional inspects the subfloor, identifies failed stumps, and assesses soil conditions
  2. Engineering specification — an engineer specifies the replacement system, sizes, and layout
  3. Jacking — the house is carefully jacked up using hydraulic jacks to relieve load on the old stumps
  4. Removal — old stumps are removed. Timber stumps are cut and extracted; concrete stumps are broken out
  5. Installation — new concrete-free stumps are installed to the specified depth and position
  6. Levelling — the house is precisely re-levelled using the adjustable stump system
  7. Completion — jacks are removed, the house sits on its new foundations, and a compliance certificate is issued

Restumping Costs in Melbourne

Restumping costs depend on several factors:

  • Number of stumps — a typical 3-bedroom Melbourne home has 40–80 stumps
  • Access difficulty — low subfloor clearance or restricted access increases cost
  • Soil conditions — reactive clay may require deeper or more robust systems
  • House size and weight — larger, heavier homes need more substantial stumps
  • Partial vs full restump — replacing some stumps costs less than a complete restump

Do You Need a Permit to Restump in Victoria?

Restumping generally requires a building permit in Victoria, especially for a full restump. Key points:

  • A full restump requires a building permit and structural engineer's report
  • A partial restump (a few stumps) may be exempt — check with your building surveyor
  • An engineer's report documents the existing condition and specifies the replacement system
  • Easy Footings provides all engineering documentation for your permit application

Choosing a Restumping Contractor in Melbourne

When selecting a restumping contractor, look for:

  • Registered builder — ensure they hold a valid Victorian building registration
  • Insurance — public liability and structural warranty insurance
  • Engineer certification — the work should be engineer-specified and certified on completion
  • Product quality — ask what system they use. BMSA products are engineer-certified to Australian standards
  • Experience — look for a track record with Melbourne's soil conditions and older home types

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does restumping cost in Melbourne?
Costs vary based on house size, number of stumps, access difficulty, and soil conditions. A typical 3-bedroom home might require 40-80 stumps. Contact Easy Footings for a free assessment and quote specific to your home.
How long does restumping take?
With concrete-free systems like StumpRite, restumping a typical Melbourne home can be completed in 1-3 days. Traditional concrete restumping often takes 1-2 weeks due to excavation and curing time.
Can I live in my house during restumping?
In most cases, yes. The work is done from underneath the house. You may experience some noise and vibration during installation, but most homeowners remain in residence throughout the process.
What causes stumps to fail in Melbourne?
Common causes include timber rot and decay, reactive clay soil movement causing concrete stumps to shift, poor drainage leading to waterlogging, and termite damage. Melbourne's reactive soils accelerate stump failure in many suburbs.
