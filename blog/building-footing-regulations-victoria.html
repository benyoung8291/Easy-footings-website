Victorian Building Footing Regulations: What Every Builder Needs to Know

A clear breakdown of building standards, soil classifications, permit requirements, and how concrete-free footing systems comply.

Published 15 January 2026 · Updated 1 April 2026

Understanding building regulations for footings in Victoria is essential for every builder, contractor, and homeowner planning construction work. Whether you're building a new home, adding an extension, constructing a deck, or restumping an older home, the footing system must comply with Australian Standards and Victorian building regulations.

This guide provides a clear overview of the key regulations, standards, and requirements that govern footing design and installation in Victoria.

Overview of Australian Building Standards for Footings

Footing design in Victoria is governed by a hierarchy of standards and codes:

  • National Construction Code (NCC) — the overarching code that sets minimum requirements for building work across Australia
  • Building Code of Australia (BCA) — Volume 2 covers residential buildings (Class 1 and 10)
  • AS 2870 — the key Australian Standard for residential slabs and footings
  • AS 1684 — Australian Standard for residential timber-framed construction
  • Victorian Building Act 1993 — the state legislation governing building work in Victoria
  • Victorian Building Regulations 2018 — detailed regulations under the Act

AS 2870: Residential Slabs and Footings

AS 2870 is the most important standard for residential footing design. It establishes a soil classification system that determines the type and depth of footings required at each building site:

ClassificationDescriptionSurface Movement
Class AStable, non-reactive (sand, rock)0 mm
Class SSlightly reactive clay0–20 mm
Class MModerately reactive clay20–40 mm
Class H1Highly reactive clay40–60 mm
Class H2Very highly reactive clay60–75 mm
Class EExtremely reactive clay>75 mm
Class PProblem sites (fill, soft soil, mine sites)Variable

The higher the soil reactivity, the more robust the footing system needs to be. Melbourne and Victoria feature the full range of soil classifications, making site-specific soil testing essential.

Soil Classification in Victoria

Victoria's soils vary dramatically by region:

  • Melbourne's western suburbs (Werribee, Melton, Tarneit, Wyndham) — predominantly Class H1 to E reactive clay
  • Melbourne's northern suburbs (Craigieburn, Mickleham, Hume) — Class H1 to H2 reactive clay
  • Melbourne's eastern suburbs — mixed, from Class M to H1
  • Bayside Melbourne — sandier soils, often Class A to S
  • Geelong — mixed coastal and clay soils
  • Ballarat & Bendigo — variable clay with some rocky subsoil
  • Gippsland — highly variable, from sandy coastal to reactive inland clay

Building Permits for Footings in Victoria

A building permit is required for most structural footing work in Victoria. This includes:

  • New house or dwelling construction
  • Home extensions and additions
  • Decks over 800mm above ground level or attached to the house
  • Full house restumping
  • Granny flats and secondary dwellings
  • Commercial building footings

To obtain a building permit for footing work, you typically need: architectural or structural plans, a geotechnical soil report, engineering specifications for the footing system, and a registered building surveyor to issue the permit.

Engineer Certification Requirements

For most footing work in Victoria, a structural engineer must:

  • Review the geotechnical soil report for the site
  • Specify the footing type, size, depth, and spacing based on the soil classification and structural loads
  • Provide engineering drawings and specifications for the building permit application
  • In some cases, inspect and certify the completed footing installation

Do Concrete-Free Footings Meet Victorian Building Standards?

Yes, absolutely. Concrete-free footing systems like RapidStump, StumpRite, and SurePile are:

  • Fully engineer-certified to Australian Standards
  • Compliant with the NCC and BCA for residential and commercial applications
  • Specified by structural engineers on a site-by-site basis, just like concrete footings
  • Accepted by building surveyors across Victoria for building permit applications
  • Load-tested and documented with full engineering data for each product

The engineer specifies the correct BMSA product, size, and installation depth based on the soil classification and structural requirements — exactly the same process as specifying concrete footings.

Footing Depth Requirements in Victoria

Minimum footing depth in Victoria depends on the soil classification:

  • Class A/S soils — minimum depths from approximately 400mm
  • Class M soils — deeper footings required, often 600mm–1m
  • Class H1/H2 soils — footings must reach below the reactive zone, typically 1.5m–3m
  • Class E soils — deep footings, often 3m–4m+ to reach stable ground
  • Class P soils — engineer-specific design required

Screw pile footing systems are particularly well-suited to deep footing requirements, as they can be mechanically driven to significant depths without the excavation and formwork challenges of deep concrete footings.

Special Considerations for Victorian Regions

Bushfire Zones (BAL Ratings)

Properties in designated bushfire-prone areas must comply with BAL (Bushfire Attack Level) ratings. Footing systems and subfloor construction may need additional protection in BAL-12.5 through BAL-FZ zones. Gippsland and parts of the Dandenong Ranges are particularly affected.

Flood-Prone Areas

Properties in flood overlays may have minimum floor height requirements that affect footing design. Concrete-free adjustable footings can accommodate elevated floor heights without complex formwork.

Steep Sites

Sloping sites require careful engineering of footing heights and lateral bracing. Adjustable concrete-free systems simplify construction on steep Victorian blocks, particularly on the Mornington Peninsula and in hill suburbs.

Common Compliance Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the soil test — never assume soil conditions. A geotechnical report is essential and usually required for permits.
  • Not getting an engineer's specification — footing design must be engineer-specified, not guessed or based on rules of thumb
  • Insufficient footing depth — footings that don't reach below the reactive zone will move with the soil
  • No building permit — building without a required permit can result in fines, enforcement action, and difficulty selling the property
  • Poor documentation — keep all engineering reports, soil tests, and compliance certificates. You'll need them for future sales or insurance claims.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a building permit for footings in Victoria?
Yes, in most cases. Any structural footing work associated with a building permit requires the footings to be included. Some minor works may be exempt — consult your building surveyor.
What is AS 2870?
AS 2870 is the Australian Standard for Residential Slabs and Footings. It classifies soil reactivity and specifies minimum footing requirements based on your site's soil classification.
Do concrete-free footings meet Victorian building standards?
Yes. RapidStump, StumpRite and SurePile are fully engineer-certified, compliant with the NCC/BCA, and accepted by building surveyors across Victoria.
Do I need a soil test before installing footings?
A geotechnical soil report is required for most building permit applications in Victoria. It classifies the soil reactivity, which determines footing type, size, and depth.
What is the minimum footing depth in Victoria?
It depends on soil classification. Stable soils (Class A/S) start at around 400mm. Highly reactive soils (H2/E) may require 1.5m to 4m+ depth. Your engineer specifies the exact depth for your site.
