What Steel House Stumps Are

Searches such as “steel house stumps”, “steel stumps” and “steel stumps for house” usually mean the same product: a galvanised steel post that sits under a bearer and carries a point load from a raised floor. The stump is the column. It is not a hardware-store pad, not a poured pier, and not a continuous strip under a wall.

Under a typical Melbourne weatherboard the stumps sit on a grid of roughly 1.2–1.8 metres. A house often has 40–80 of them. The same geometry shows up under raised shop floors, light-commercial verandahs, decks and rear extensions — any structure that is held up on isolated supports rather than a slab. That is why this page is a Victorian house-and-deck answer, not a product catalogue.

Galvanised vs Adjustable Steel Stumps

Those two words answer different questions. People searching “galvanised steel house stumps” are asking about corrosion protection. People searching “adjustable steel house stumps” or “adjustable steel stumps” are asking about re-levelling. Most steel house stumps we install in 2026 are both.

Term What it means Why it matters in Victoria
Galvanised steel house stump A steel post with a zinc coating, typically hot-dip galvanised for ground contact Resists rust in damp sub-floors and reactive clay better than bare or painted steel
Adjustable steel house stump A galvanised post with a threaded head; StumpRite typically gives up to 120 mm of screw height adjustment You set the floor at install, then wind individual stumps later if clay heaves or shrinks
Fixed-height steel post Galvanised, but no screw head Cannot be re-levelled from the top; same limitation as a concrete stump once the clay moves

Galvanising is the coating. Adjustability is the head. A cheap uncoated post under a Melbourne house is the wrong product even if it is steel. A galvanised post without a screw head is still the wrong product on Class M to H2 clay if you expect to re-level later. Licensed install of the adjustable range: adjustable stumps in Victoria.

When Steel Stumps Are Used: Restumping, New Builds and Decks

The same steel post shows up in three Victorian jobs. The engineering and the permit path change; the material does not.

  • House restumping. The usual call: failed timber or concrete under a raised weatherboard, Federation house or later extension. The house is jacked, old stumps come out, new steel goes in, the floor is levelled. Full process: Melbourne restumping guide.
  • New-build raised floors. New houses, granny flats and light-commercial floors designed as a stumped frame rather than a slab. Driven or adjustable steel avoids a concrete pour and a curing wait.
  • Decks, verandahs and rear extensions. Isolated posts under a deck or a raised addition. Wind uplift still matters. See the deck footing installation guide.

Steel stumps are a poor fit when the engineer wants a continuous strip under a masonry wall, or a helical pile below the active zone for a heavy modular or container load. That second job is SurePile, not a house stump.

Melbourne Clay and AS 2870

In Victoria, house supports are designed to AS 2870-2011 Residential slabs and footings from a geotechnical soil test. The test assigns a site class. The class sets how much seasonal surface movement the engineer must design for. Full explanation: AS 2870 explained.

Much of Melbourne’s west and north — Werribee, Melton, Tarneit, Point Cook and similar basaltic clay corridors — classifies M to H2. Class M is the most common suburban Melbourne result; H1 and H2 are routine on the western plains. Large parts of regional Victoria do the same: Geelong, Ballarat, Bendigo, the Mornington Peninsula and Gippsland all see reactive clay. Trees, fill or abnormal moisture can push a site to Class P.

Reactive clay is why adjustable steel wins the 2026 restump. A shallow isolated support in the seasonal active zone heaves and shrinks with the clay. Each support can move on its own (differential settlement). Timber packing wears out. A concrete stump cannot be re-levelled without jacking the house and digging it out. An adjustable galvanised head can be wound from the top after the season moves. Expected surface-movement bands already used on our AS 2870 pages: A 0–10 mm, S 10–20 mm, M 20–40 mm, H1 40–60 mm, H2 60–75 mm, E 75 mm+. Those are characteristic values for classification, not a promise of how far your house will move.

Steel vs Timber vs Concrete (Brief)

Installed restumping sits in the same 2026 Melbourne band whichever material you choose. The difference is what you get, not a separate price list. Short version:

  • Timber is cheaper as a supply-only post and still the most common failed stump under older Melbourne weatherboards. It rots and is a termite food source.
  • Concrete is durable if it stays intact, but it can crack or spall and cannot be re-levelled without excavation. See concrete stumps Melbourne.
  • Adjustable galvanised steel lasts longer, is not a food source, and can be re-levelled from the top after seasonal clay movement. That is the usual 2026 upgrade.

Building Permit Path in Victoria

House restumping and new house stumps are structural building work in Victoria. They generally require a building permit issued by a registered building surveyor (council or private), whether the new stumps are timber, concrete or steel. Footing design may need to be certified by a structural engineer, especially on reactive clay. A soil test classifying the site under AS 2870 is often part of that pack.

Class 10 accessory structures (some decks, sheds and garden structures) can have a different path. Size, siting and whether the structure is attached to a dwelling all matter. A licensed building surveyor decides any exemption. Easy Footings does not issue permits. Confirm the exact path with your surveyor before work starts. How Steel House Stumps Are Installed

Concrete-free steel restumping does not wait on a pour. The house is typically lowered the same visit. The sequence matches the Melbourne restumping guide:

  1. Inspection. An assessor maps failed stumps, access under the floor, and whether the job is a full or partial restump.
  2. Quote and engineering. Soil class, stump count and the BMSA system (StumpRite, RapidStump or SurePile) go into the quote. A surveyor handles the permit.
  3. Jacking. Hydraulic jacks lift the house off the existing stumps, progressively, so the frame is not racked.
  4. Old stump removal. Failed timber is pulled or cut; concrete may be broken out.
  5. New steel installation. StumpRite is set with its adjustable head. RapidStump is driven as a footing and stump in one. SurePile is wound to a verified torque where a pile is the engineered answer.
  6. Levelling and lowering. Bearers sit on the new heads. The house is lowered and secured. Adjustable heads can be wound again later if clay moves.

House restumping is licensed professional work. Owner-builders can supply and install some BMSA components on simple Class 10 jobs with engineer drawings — see DIY vs professional footing installation in Victoria. Easy Footings supplies only, or supplies and installs.

Steel House Stump FAQs

What are steel house stumps?

Steel house stumps (also searched as steel stumps or steel stumps for house) are galvanised steel posts used under raised houses, decks and extensions. Many have an adjustable threaded head so the floor can be re-levelled later. They are the column supporting the bearer, not a hardware-store pad or a poured concrete pier.

What is the difference between galvanised steel house stumps and adjustable steel stumps?

Galvanised is the corrosion coating — typically hot-dip galvanised for Victorian ground contact. Adjustable is the threaded head, typically up to 120 mm of screw height adjustment on StumpRite. Most steel house stumps Easy Footings installs are both. A fixed-height galvanised post without a screw head cannot be re-levelled from the top after clay movement.

How much do steel stumps for a house cost in Melbourne?

Installed restumping typically costs $500–$1,000+ per stump, $5,000–$15,000 for a partial restump, or $20,000–$50,000 for a full restump (2026 Melbourne bands). Supply-only StumpRite starts from $10.35 ex GST. Those figures are indicative. See the adjustable stump cost guide and the restumping cost guide.

Are steel house stumps better than timber or concrete on Melbourne clay?

Steel lasts longer and resists termites, rot and fire better than timber. Concrete is durable if it stays intact, but it can crack or spall and cannot be re-levelled without excavation. Adjustable galvanised steel can be re-levelled from the top after seasonal clay movement. Full table: steel vs timber vs concrete stumps in Victoria.

Do you need a building permit to install steel house stumps in Victoria?

House restumping and new house stumps generally require a building permit from a registered building surveyor. Class 10 accessory structures may have a different path; the surveyor decides. Confirm before you start — see the restumping permit guide. This is not legal advice.
