What Adjustable House Stumps Are

Adjustable house stumps are steel house stumps with a screw head. On StumpRite — Easy Footings’ Australian-made, hot-dip galvanised range — that head typically gives up to 120 mm of screw height adjustment. You set the floor level at install, then wind individual stumps later if Melbourne clay heaves or shrinks. No concrete is poured under the stump.

That is a different product from a $10 base plate. Searches such as “adjustable house stumps price” and “adjustable steel house stumps” usually mean the installed restump or new-stump job, not a supply-only SKU. Licensed install across Melbourne and Victoria is covered on the adjustable stumps installation page.

Timber stumps rot and feed termites. Precast concrete stumps are rigid columns: they can crack or spall, and they cannot be re-levelled without jacking the house and digging them out. Adjustable steel sits in the middle of that problem: durable like steel, re-levelled from the top after seasonal movement. This page is the price guide. What Drives Adjustable Stump Cost

The same factors move a restump quote on the Melbourne restumping guide. For adjustable steel they show up as:

  • Stump count. A typical weatherboard has 40–80 stumps. A partial restump of 10–20 is a different job from a full house.
  • Access under the floor. Low clearance, tight sub-floor space and services that have to be moved all add labour. Compact gear helps; it does not make a crawl-space job cheap.
  • AS 2870 soil class. Much of Melbourne’s west and north sits on reactive clay, commonly Class H1 or H2. More reactive sites need a soil test, engineer drawings and a founding depth that suits the class — see AS 2870 explained.
  • Extra height and bracing. Taller stumps and StumpRite cross bracing (listed at $87.40 ex GST supply-only) add to both the parts list and the install time.
  • Restump vs new-build. Replacing failed stumps under an occupied house is jacking and removal. New-build stumps on an empty frame are usually faster.
  • Regional Victoria travel. Per-stump rates in Geelong, Ballarat, Bendigo, the Mornington Peninsula, Gippsland and the Yarra Ranges are broadly in line with Melbourne, but travel and mobilisation can shift a quote.

Adjustable Steel vs Concrete vs Timber

Installed restumping sits in the same 2026 band whichever material you choose: $500–$1,000+ per stump, or $20,000–$50,000 for a full restump. The difference is what you get for that money, not a separate price list. Short version:

  • Timber is cheaper as a supply-only post and still the most common failed stump under older Melbourne weatherboards. It rots and is a termite food source.
  • Concrete is durable if it stays intact, but it can crack or spall and cannot be re-levelled without excavation. See concrete stumps Melbourne.
  • Adjustable galvanised steel (StumpRite) is the usual 2026 upgrade on Victorian clay because the head can be re-levelled from the top after seasonal movement. Licensed install: adjustable stumps Victoria.

Building Permit / Restumping Permit in Victoria

Restumping is structural building work in Victoria. It generally requires a building permit issued by a registered building surveyor (council or private), whether the new stumps are timber, concrete or adjustable steel. Footing design may need engineer certification, especially on reactive clay. A soil test classifying the site under AS 2870 is often part of that pack.

This is a practical overview, not legal advice. A licensed building surveyor decides what your job needs. Easy Footings does not issue permits. For the plain-English path, see do you need a building permit to restump in Victoria.

StumpRite is the adjustable house-stump answer: screw height adjustment, hot-dip galvanised, Australian-made, restump or new stumps where you want to re-level later. It is not the only BMSA system Easy Footings installs.

Are adjustable steel house stumps AS 2870 compliant?

Replacement and new house stumps in Victoria must be designed to suit the site classification under AS 2870 from a soil test. StumpRite is an Australian-made, engineer-certified steel stump system. The soil class, engineer drawings and building surveyor decide the exact layout. A product name alone is not a substitute for a site-specific design.
