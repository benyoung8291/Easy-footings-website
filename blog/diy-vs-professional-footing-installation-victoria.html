DIY vs Professional Footing Installation in Victoria

When DIY or Owner-Builder Supply-Only Is Reasonable

Supply-only is a genuine option. Easy Footings sells BMSA Footing Solutions components so an owner-builder or experienced tradesperson can install them on a simple job. The usual fit is a Class 10 accessory structure — a deck, garden shed, carport or similar non-habitable building — where a structural engineer has already issued drawings and your building surveyor has accepted who will install the footings.

That still means following the drawings: the specified product, depth, spacing and connections. A screw pile or driven stump that stops short of the design depth, or is wound in without meeting the specified torque, is not the footing the engineer certified. Soil class matters as much as the steel. A Class A or S sandy site is a different install from Melbourne’s reactive clay.

Reasonable DIY or owner-builder territory looks like this:

  • A low-level deck or shed on a relatively level block, with engineer drawings in hand
  • You (or your carpenter) can handle the driver or jackhammer and the manufacturer’s install method
  • Services have been located, and you are not working under a live house
  • The building surveyor has confirmed any permit, exemption or owner-builder consent that applies

For deck detailing see the deck footing installation guide. For sheds, see shed footings in Victoria. Product pages list SKU prices and 14-day returns on unused goods — see the purchase terms for supply-only conditions. Easy Footings does not accept liability for third-party installation of supply-only products; that responsibility sits with the person who installs them.

When You Must Use a Professional

Hire a licensed professional stump installer for anything that holds up a house, or that a surveyor will treat as structural work needing a permit and an engineer certificate. In practice that is most jobs people search for when they type “install stumps yourself Victoria”.

  • House restumping (full or partial). The house has to be jacked, old stumps removed, and the structure lowered onto new ones. That is not a weekend job. See the Melbourne restumping cost guide.
  • New dwelling stumps — Class 1 houses and new stumped floors.
  • Granny flats and secondary dwellings, which are habitable buildings, not Class 10 sheds. See granny flat footings in Victoria.
  • Reactive clay (much of Melbourne’s west and north, and large parts of regional Victoria). Depth and torque have to match the soil class or the stump will heave or settle. Adjustable steel is the usual professional upgrade on clay, not a DIY guess.
  • Sloping blocks, where pile lengths and cut-and-fill change from grid to grid.
  • Work near trees, where root zones and Tree Protection Zones constrain how you found.
  • BAL-rated (bushfire) sites, where the footing and stump specification is part of the BAL package.
  • Adjoining property — footings on or near a boundary, or work that could undermine a neighbour’s footing or fence. Victorian building work that protects adjoining property is a common permit trigger; do not assume an exemption.

If you are unsure which side of the line you are on, treat it as a professional job and ask a building surveyor. That is cheaper than pulling uncertified stumps later.

Permits, Engineers and the VBA

This is a high-level overview, not legal advice. Confirm the path for your site with a registered building surveyor (council or private) and, where required, the Victorian Building Authority (VBA) owner-builder process.

In Victoria, most building work involving footings needs a building permit before work starts. That includes new homes, extensions, most elevated decks, restumping and relevelling, and sheds or outbuildings over the size and siting thresholds. Some minor works can be exempt — a small garden shed or a very low deck, for example — but thresholds vary by council. Do not assume an exemption.

The surveyor issues the permit, inspects the work, and (for restumping) typically issues a certificate of final inspection. Footing design may need a structural engineer certificate, especially on reactive clay or where an alternative (non-deemed-to-comply) system is used. A soil test may be required if the property does not already have one. Replacement and new stumps must be designed for the site classification under AS 2870.

Owner-builders in Victoria may need owner-builder consent for work above certain cost or complexity thresholds. The VBA publishes the current rules; we do not restate dollar limits here because they change. Ask your surveyor what consent, if any, applies before you order steel.

For the permit and certification picture in plain English, see Victorian building footing regulations and AS 2870 explained.

Risks of DIY Restumping

DIY restumping is the job we are asked about most often, and the one we advise against. The risks are structural, not cosmetic.

  • Jacking. The house is lifted off the old stumps with hydraulic jacks, progressively and in sequence. Uneven lift cracks plaster, twists frames, and in the worst case drops a section of floor.
  • Services. Plumbing, gas, electrical and sewer lines run under most Melbourne weatherboards. A jack, a driven pile or a broken concrete stump can hit them.
  • Collapse. Temporary supports that are short, soft or badly placed fail. That is how people are injured under houses.
  • Wrong depth, torque or soil class. A stump that looks “in” but has not reached the engineer’s founding layer will settle or heave with the next wet winter. That is the usual failure mode, not the steel itself.
  • Insurance. Stump deterioration from age, rot or gradual soil movement is treated as wear and tear and is excluded by most Australian home policies. Unlicensed or unpermitted structural work can also leave you uninsured if something fails. Check your PDS and talk to your insurer; do not assume cover.

Restumping is also the job where a professional crew finishes in days with the right gear. Concrete-free driven steel can be lowered the same day. The saving from doing it yourself is small against a failed floor or a stop-work order.

DIY vs Professional FAQs

Can I install screw piles or stumps myself in Victoria?

On some simple Class 10 jobs — a deck, shed or other accessory structure with engineer drawings — an owner-builder can supply and install BMSA components themselves. House restumping and anything that needs a building permit or engineer certificate should be installed by a licensed professional. Wrong depth, torque or soil class is how stumps fail.

Can an owner-builder install BMSA footings in Victoria?

Yes, on simple Class 10 work where the surveyor and engineer have accepted owner-builder installation and you follow the certified drawings. Easy Footings can supply RapidStump, StumpRite or SurePile only. Confirm owner-builder consent and permit conditions with your building surveyor before you start.

Is house restumping a DIY job in Victoria?

No. Restumping is structural building work. It generally requires a building permit, and the house has to be jacked, levelled and lowered onto new stumps. Getting jacking, services or soil class wrong can collapse part of the floor. Use a licensed professional stump installer. Cost and process are in the restumping guide.

Do you need a building permit to restump a house in Victoria?

Yes. Restumping is structural work and generally requires a building permit from a registered building surveyor. Footing design may need an engineer certificate, especially on reactive clay. Confirm requirements with your surveyor before work starts — see the regulations guide.

How much does professional restumping cost compared with buying the stumps?

Installed restumping typically costs $500–$1,000+ per stump. A full restump is $20,000–$50,000; a partial restump of 10–20 stumps is $5,000–$15,000. Product SKU prices are components only — RapidStump is listed at $122 ex GST on the product page, which is not an install price.

Will home insurance cover a failed DIY restump?

Usually not. Stump deterioration is treated as wear and tear. Unlicensed or unpermitted structural work can also void cover if something fails. Check your PDS and talk to your insurer before assuming either way.

When should I hire a professional stump installer in Melbourne?

Hire a professional for house restumping, new dwelling stumps, granny flats, reactive clay, sloping blocks, work near trees, BAL-rated sites, and anything near an adjoining property. Those jobs need the right depth, torque and soil class, plus permit and engineer paperwork.
