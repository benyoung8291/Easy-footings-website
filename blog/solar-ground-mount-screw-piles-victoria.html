Screw Pile Footings for Solar Ground-Mount Arrays in Regional Victoria

By Easy Footings • 17 April 2026 • 7 min read

From small off-grid paddock arrays on Gippsland farms to mid-scale commercial PV installations across the Wimmera, ground-mount solar is booming in regional Victoria. And almost everyone — from EPC contractors to off-grid homeowners — has quietly landed on the same answer for the footings: screw piles. Here's why screw piles dominate ground-mount solar foundations in Victoria, how they're specified, and what makes them especially well suited to our soil profile.

Why the solar industry chose screw piles

Ground-mount solar has four hard foundation requirements: fast install across hundreds or thousands of points, precision vertical alignment, uplift capacity against wind and tracker loads, and removability at end of life. Concrete piers struggle with all four. Screw piles tick every box.

  • Installation speed — a two-person crew with the right drive head can install 60 to 120 piles per day, depending on soil. Concrete is an order of magnitude slower.
  • No curing — the racking can be bolted up the same hour the pile goes in.
  • Torque-verified capacity — every pile produces a live load-capacity signal via installation torque, documented per footing.
  • Reversibility — at end of system life, piles unscrew out of the ground. No demolition, no landfill, no buried concrete liability on the title.
  • Zero wet trades — no water, no mixer, no contamination risk in protected agricultural land.

Where screw piles are used across regional Victoria

  • Off-grid homesteads — 10 to 40 kW ground-mount arrays on rural blocks in Macedon, Daylesford, Strathbogie, Gippsland and the Otways.
  • Farm energy independence — irrigation-powering arrays for horticulture and dairy operations across the Goulburn Valley and Western District.
  • Behind-the-meter commercial solar — industrial and warehouse paddock arrays in Shepparton, Bendigo and Mildura-region sites.
  • Utility-scale PV pre-feasibility — pilot arrays that need to be installed, tested and removed without leaving a concrete legacy.
  • EV charging canopies — single-row ground-mount structures covering parking bays at rural service stops.

Matching pile design to Victorian soils

Regional Victoria covers an enormous range of soils. A single specification rarely works across a whole state, so pile design is always matched to the site:

  • Wimmera and Mallee black-earth clays — highly reactive. Piles are driven to embed below the seasonal zone, typically 2 to 3 metres, with single or double helical plates for lateral stability.
  • Basalt plains of the Western District — strong at depth but often shallow rock. Piles are shorter, high-torque, with plate sizes chosen to bear on the weathered basalt profile.
  • Goulburn Valley alluvial loams — good bearing with consistent depth behaviour. Standard helical pile designs perform excellently here.
  • Sandy coastal plains (Ninety Mile Beach, Nullawarre) — friction-bearing piles with larger helical plates to resist uplift on loose sand.
  • East Gippsland granite and metamorphic — variable depth-to-rock; pile lengths are adjusted per row, which is trivial with screw piles and expensive with bored concrete piers.

Engineering and certification

A ground-mount solar installation in Victoria needs a properly engineered foundation regardless of scale. For installations, that means:

  1. Racking supplier loads — axial, lateral and uplift loads are provided by the tracker or fixed-tilt racking manufacturer.
  2. Geotechnical investigation — test piles or boreholes characterise the site and validate design assumptions. Large sites use pull-tests to prove capacity.
  3. Pile schedule — the engineer issues a schedule of pile lengths, helical plate sizes, target torques and embedment depths.
  4. Install-phase QA — each pile's installation torque and depth are logged. Outliers are investigated, piles are re-driven or replaced as needed.
  5. As-built certification — a statement of compliance is issued covering the pile array as built.

Installation productivity in real numbers

  • Small off-grid 10 kW array (roughly 24 panels, 6 to 8 piles) — installed in under half a day.
  • Farm-scale 100 kW array (240 panels, 40 to 60 piles) — one to two days.
  • Behind-the-meter 500 kW array (1,000+ panels, 200 to 300 piles) — typically a single week with one rig.
  • Equivalent bored concrete piers — two to three times the duration, plus curing time before racking can bolt up.

Environmental and compliance advantages

  • No concrete means no embodied carbon from the foundations, often a meaningful improvement in lifecycle emissions for the project.
  • Minimal spoil — soils stay in place, no excavation piles, no landform disturbance.
  • Reversible at end of life, supporting council and landowner commitments to site restoration.
  • Compatible with agrivoltaic designs where grazing continues beneath the panels.
  • Works around existing infrastructure — irrigation lines, native vegetation zones, waterway setbacks.
