Steel vs Timber vs Concrete Stumps in Victoria: Which Should You Use?

By Easy Footings • 26 August 2026 • 8 min read

Steel stumps last longer and resist termites, rot and fire better than timber. Adjustable galvanised steel — StumpRite or RapidStump — is the usual 2026 upgrade when restumping in Victoria. Timber is cheaper upfront. Concrete is durable, but it can crack or spall and cannot be re-levelled without excavation. This guide compares the three options for Melbourne and regional Victorian homes.

Timber vs Concrete vs Steel House Stumps

Most older Melbourne weatherboards still sit on timber or concrete stumps. When those fail, the replacement choice is timber, concrete or steel. For how the job itself is priced and sequenced, see our Melbourne restumping cost guide.

Timber is not a bad material in the right conditions. The problem in Victoria is the combination of damp sub-floors, termites and reactive clay. Those three things are why timber stumps keep coming out of Melbourne houses, and why putting timber back in is rarely the long-term choice.

Why Melbourne Clay Makes Adjustable Steel the Better Fit

Much of Melbourne’s west and north, and large parts of regional Victoria, sit on reactive clay. As that clay wets in winter and dries in summer it swells and shrinks. AS 2870 classifies that movement from Class M through H2, and in some inland pockets Class E. Fixed stumps sitting in the active layer move with it.

That seasonal heave is what produces sloping floors, sticking doors and diagonal plaster cracks years after a restump. With timber, the packing compresses and the stump itself can rot at ground line. With concrete, the stump is a rigid column: when the clay lifts one corner of the house, there is no way to wind that stump back down. Re-levelling means jacking the house and digging the stump out.

Adjustable galvanised steel changes that. Driven or screwed systems can found below the reactive surface layer, so they are less affected by the seasonal wetting and drying. A threaded stump head — up to 120 mm on StumpRite — then lets the floor be re-levelled later with hand tools, without excavation. That is the practical reason adjustable steel is the usual restumping upgrade on Victorian clay, not a marketing distinction.

What Restumping with Steel Costs

Stay with the same 2026 ranges used for restumping across Melbourne and regional Victoria:

  • Per stump: $500–$1,000+ (supply and install)
  • Partial restump (10–20 stumps): $5,000–$15,000
  • Full restump (typical weatherboard, 40–60 stumps): $20,000–$50,000

Steel often costs more per stump than timber. That gap is the price of not doing the job again in 20–40 years, and of being able to re-level after clay movement instead of excavating. Access, soil class, engineering and whether you choose driven RapidStump, adjustable StumpRite or helical SurePile all move a quote within that band. We do not publish tighter per-stump prices because sites vary too much for a single figure to be honest.

Concrete-free driven steel can still be competitive on total job cost because there is no concrete delivery, no 7–28 day cure, and no return visit to lower the house. Full cost breakdown, warning signs and timeframes are in the restumping cost guide.

Permits and Engineer Certification

Restumping is structural building work in Victoria. It generally requires a building permit issued by a registered building surveyor (council or private), and the completed work is inspected before a certificate of final inspection is issued. Footing design may need to be certified by a structural engineer, particularly on reactive clay or where an alternative (non-deemed-to-comply) system is used.

A soil test may be required if the property does not already have one. Replacement stumps must be designed to suit the site classification under AS 2870. That applies whether the new stumps are timber, concrete or steel — the material does not remove the permit obligation.

Confirm the exact path with your building surveyor before work starts. For a plain-English overview of permits, soil class and engineer certification, see our Victorian building footing regulations guide and AS 2870 explained.

Steel Stump FAQs

Are steel stumps better than timber?

For most Victorian homes, yes. Galvanised steel lasts longer and resists termites, rot and fire better than timber. Timber is cheaper upfront, but that saving is often lost when the house needs restumping again in 20–40 years.

Are steel stumps better than concrete?

Steel and concrete are both durable. Concrete can crack or spall, and it cannot be re-levelled without excavation. Adjustable galvanised steel can be re-levelled from the top after seasonal clay movement, which is why it is the usual 2026 upgrade when restumping in Victoria.

How much do steel stumps cost in Melbourne?

Restumping typically costs $500–$1,000+ per stump in 2026. A full restump of an average weatherboard is $20,000–$50,000; a partial restump of 10–20 stumps is $5,000–$15,000. Steel often costs more per stump than timber, but avoids another restump in 20–40 years.

Do steel stumps rust under a Melbourne house?

Hot-dip galvanised steel is specified for Australian conditions, including under-floor damp. Sub-floor ventilation and drainage still matter, as they do for any stump system. Untreated or poorly coated steel is not what is used for house restumping.

Can steel house stumps be adjusted after installation?

Yes, if you specify an adjustable system. StumpRite provides up to 120 mm of screw height adjustment, so floors can be re-levelled later without digging the stump out. Fixed timber and concrete stumps cannot.

Do you need a building permit to restump with steel in Victoria?

Restumping is structural work and generally requires a building permit from a registered building surveyor. Footing design may need engineer certification, especially on reactive clay. Confirm requirements with your surveyor before work starts — see the regulations guide.

Why are adjustable steel stumps recommended on Melbourne clay?

Reactive clay swells in winter and shrinks in summer. Fixed timber or concrete stumps move with that heave. Adjustable galvanised steel can be re-levelled from the top after seasonal movement, without excavating the stump.
