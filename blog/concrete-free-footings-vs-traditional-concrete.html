Concrete-Free Footings vs Traditional Concrete: The Complete Comparison for Victorian Builders

A detailed side-by-side comparison to help Melbourne and Victorian builders choose the right foundation system for their next project.

Published 15 March 2026 · Updated 1 April 2026

If you're a builder, contractor, or homeowner planning a construction project in Melbourne or regional Victoria, choosing the right footing system is one of the most important decisions you'll make. For decades, traditional poured concrete footings have been the default. But a new generation of concrete-free footing systems — including RapidStump, StumpRite, and SurePile from BMSA Footing Solutions — is rapidly changing the way Victorian builders approach foundations.

In this comprehensive guide, we compare both options across every factor that matters: installation speed, weather resistance, cost, environmental impact, engineering certification, and suitability for different project types. By the end, you'll have a clear picture of which system is right for your next build.

What Are Concrete-Free Footings?

Concrete-free footings are engineered foundation systems that are mechanically driven or screwed into the ground without the need to pour concrete. They use steel components — helical screw piles, driven stumps, or bracket-anchored posts — to achieve the required load-bearing capacity.

Easy Footings installs three BMSA systems: RapidStump for fast, versatile installations; StumpRite for precision restumping of older homes; and SurePile for heavy-duty screw pile applications. All three are engineer-certified and compliant with Australian building standards.

What Are Traditional Concrete Footings?

Traditional concrete footings involve excavating holes or trenches, placing reinforcing steel (rebar), pouring wet concrete, and then waiting for it to cure before any construction can begin on top. This has been standard practice in Australian residential and commercial building for over a century.

While concrete is a proven material, the process of pouring and curing it introduces significant delays, weather dependencies, and environmental costs that modern builders are increasingly looking to avoid.

Installation Speed: Build Immediately vs Wait Weeks

This is the single biggest advantage of concrete-free footings. Traditional concrete footings require excavation, formwork, steel placement, pouring, and then a mandatory curing period of 7 to 28 days before you can place any load on them. During Melbourne's cooler months, curing can take even longer.

Concrete-free systems like RapidStump can be installed in a fraction of the time, and — critically — you can start building the moment installation is complete. For a typical residential deck, that might mean footings installed and a builder on-site the same day, rather than waiting weeks for concrete to reach strength.

For builders managing multiple projects and tight schedules, this time saving translates directly into more revenue and better client satisfaction.

Weather Resistance: Why Melbourne's Climate Matters

Melbourne is famous for its unpredictable weather. The city averages over 140 rainy days per year, and Victorian winters regularly bring temperatures that affect concrete curing. When it rains, concrete pours must be postponed. When temperatures drop below 5°C, curing slows dramatically and can compromise strength.

Concrete-free footings eliminate this variable entirely. Screw pile systems can be installed in rain, cold, heat, or wind — the weather simply doesn't matter. For builders working through Melbourne's winter months, this alone can save weeks of delays across a building season.

Environmental Impact: The Carbon Footprint Difference

Concrete production is responsible for approximately 8% of global CO₂ emissions. The cement manufacturing process releases massive amounts of carbon dioxide, and the logistics of mixing, transporting, and pouring concrete on-site add further to the environmental cost.

Concrete-free footings use zero concrete, which means zero concrete-related carbon emissions. The steel components are durable, long-lasting, and recyclable at end of life. For builders and homeowners committed to reducing their environmental impact, this is a compelling advantage.

Cost Comparison: Total Project Savings

When comparing costs, many people look only at the per-footing material price. But the total project cost is what matters, and this is where concrete-free footings deliver significant savings:

  • Reduced labour time — faster installation means fewer labour hours on-site
  • No concrete truck costs — no ordering, scheduling, or paying for concrete delivery
  • Zero weather delays — no lost days waiting for dry weather or re-booking trades
  • No formwork or excavation — less site preparation and cleanup
  • Immediate follow-on trades — framing, decking, or other work can start the same day

When you factor in these savings, concrete-free footings are typically more cost-effective overall, even where the material cost per footing is comparable to concrete.

When to Choose Concrete-Free Footings

Concrete-free footings are the ideal choice for a wide range of Victorian building projects:

  • Deck footings — fast installation, adjustable height, minimal garden disruption
  • Home extensions — add footings adjacent to existing structures without vibration or wet trades
  • House restumping — replace old timber or concrete stumps with modern adjustable systems
  • Granny flats & secondary dwellings — speed up construction timelines
  • Sheds & outbuildings — simple, fast foundation for rural and suburban structures
  • Sloping sites — adjustable height accommodates uneven terrain without complex formwork
  • Bushfire rebuilds — rapid deployment in areas where access and speed are critical

When Traditional Concrete May Still Be Required

There are some situations where concrete footings remain the preferred or required option:

  • Continuous strip footings — for masonry walls requiring continuous support
  • Slab-on-ground construction — where a concrete slab is the floor system
  • Very high load applications — some heavy commercial or industrial structures
  • Specific engineering requirements — where an engineer specifies concrete for site-specific reasons

That said, many projects that traditionally used concrete can now use concrete-free alternatives. Always consult with a structural engineer to determine the best system for your specific project. Learn more about Victorian footing regulations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are concrete-free footings as strong as concrete?
Yes. Concrete-free footing systems like RapidStump, StumpRite and SurePile are fully engineer-certified and meet or exceed Australian building standards. They are load-tested and specified by structural engineers for each project.
How much faster are concrete-free footings than concrete?
Concrete-free footings are installed up to 3x faster than traditional concrete footings. More importantly, there is zero curing time — you can build immediately after installation, saving days or weeks on your project timeline.
Can concrete-free footings be used for house foundations?
Yes. Concrete-free systems are used for decks, extensions, new builds, restumping, granny flats, sheds, and commercial structures. Your engineer will specify the right system based on load requirements and soil conditions.
Do concrete-free footings work in Melbourne's reactive clay soils?
Absolutely. Systems like SurePile are specifically designed to perform in reactive clay soils (Class H and E) common across Melbourne's western and northern suburbs. Engineers specify the correct system and depth for your soil classification.
What is the cost difference between concrete and concrete-free footings?
While the per-footing material cost can be similar, concrete-free footings typically deliver significant total project savings through reduced labour time, zero weather delays, no concrete truck costs, and the ability to begin building immediately without waiting for curing.
