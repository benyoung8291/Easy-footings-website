Do You Need a Building Permit to Restump in Victoria?

By Easy Footings • 27 August 2026 • 8 min read

In Victoria, house restumping almost always needs a building permit because it is building work that affects the structure. Simple like-for-like replacement of a few accessory-structure stumps can sometimes sit under exemptions, but a licensed building surveyor decides that, not a Google article. When a Restumping Permit Is Typically Required

A building permit is typically required for dwelling restumping in Victoria. Confirm that with a registered building surveyor before anyone jacks the house. This is a practical overview, not legal advice, and it is not a substitute for the surveyor’s decision on your site.

Restumping (also called reblocking) means replacing the stumps that carry the floor. On a house, that is structural work: the building is lifted, old supports come out, new ones go in, and the house is re-levelled onto them. In Victoria, work that affects the structure of a dwelling is treated as building work. That is why a house restumping permit in Melbourne, Geelong, Ballarat, Bendigo, Gippsland or elsewhere in the state is the usual path, not an optional extra.

Treat a permit as the default when any of the following apply:

  • House restumping — full or partial replacement of stumps under a Class 1 dwelling, including typical Melbourne weatherboards.
  • Changing stump type — timber to steel, concrete to driven steel, or any change from the existing system. That is not a like-for-like swap of the same member.
  • Re-levelling a dwelling — jacking and packing or replacing stumps so the floor comes back to level. The house moves; that is structural.
  • Work that affects the structure — anything that temporarily or permanently changes how the building is supported, including restumping combined with bearer or joist repairs.

The same default applies to new dwelling stumps and to habitable buildings such as granny flats. For the wider permit and certification picture, see Victorian building footing regulations. Replacement stumps still have to suit the site classification under AS 2870.

Starting without a permit, when one is required, can mean a stop-work order, inspection problems, and difficulty selling or insuring the property later. Ask the surveyor first.

When a Permit Might Not Be Required

Some minor work can sit under building-permit exemptions. The usual conversation is about a minor Class 10 accessory structure — a small shed, a low deck, a carport or similar non-habitable building — and a simple like-for-like replacement of a few stumps of the same type, in the same positions, without changing the structure or re-levelling a dwelling.

That is as far as this article will go. We are not publishing a legal exemption list, dollar thresholds, height limits, or council-by-council rules. Those change, and they are applied to the facts of the site. A licensed building surveyor decides whether an exemption applies. The Victorian Building Authority (VBA) publishes practitioner and owner-builder information; it is not a substitute for the surveyor on your job.

Do not assume an exemption because:

  • You are only replacing “a few” house stumps
  • You are swapping timber for steel and calling it maintenance
  • A neighbour restumped without paperwork (that does not make it lawful)
  • The work is in a regional council area rather than Melbourne

If the structure is a house, treat a permit as required until a registered building surveyor says otherwise. For when owner-builder supply-only is reasonable on simple Class 10 jobs versus when restumping must be professional, see DIY vs professional footing installation in Victoria.

Who Issues the Permit — and What the VBA Does

In Victoria, a registered building surveyor issues the building permit. You can appoint:

  • A municipal building surveyor through your local council, or
  • A private registered building surveyor.

Either path is valid. The surveyor reviews the application, issues the permit, inspects the work (including, for restumping, typically a certificate of final inspection), and is the person who decides whether an exemption applies. We do not name councils or quote their fees here; those details belong on the council or surveyor’s own materials.

Searches for “VBA restumping” often mix up two offices. The Victorian Building Authority regulates building practitioners and the owner-builder process. It does not typically issue the building permit for a restump. If you are an owner-builder, your surveyor will tell you whether VBA owner-builder consent is also required. We do not restate cost or complexity thresholds because they change.

Documents Surveyors Typically Ask For

Exact paperwork depends on the site and the surveyor. At a high level, restumping applications commonly include:

  • Soil report / site classification. A geotechnical report classifying the site under AS 2870 (A, S, M, H1, H2, E or P). If the property already has a current soil test, the engineer and surveyor may use it; if not, one is often required. Reactive clay across much of Melbourne’s west and north, and large parts of regional Victoria, is why this matters. See AS 2870 explained.
  • Engineer drawings. A structural engineer specifies stump type, founding depth, spacing, connections and any bracing. Driven steel, adjustable steel and helical piles are alternative systems; they still need to be certified for the loads and the soil class.
  • Existing conditions. An under-floor inspection, stump plan, photos, and notes on failed or settled stumps, clearance, and services. The surveyor and engineer need to know what is there now, not only what you intend to install.

Your surveyor may also want title information, a site plan, and the builder or installer details. Ask them for their list before you order steel. Confirm requirements with a registered building surveyor; this page is not that list.

Restumping Permit FAQs

Do I need a building permit to restump a house in Victoria?

Typically yes. House restumping is building work that affects the structure, so a building permit is almost always required. Confirm with a registered building surveyor before work starts.

Does house restumping in Melbourne need a building permit?

Yes. Dwelling restumping in Melbourne typically needs a building permit, the same as elsewhere in Victoria. A registered building surveyor — municipal or private — issues it and inspects the completed work. Do not start on the assumption that it is maintenance.

When might restumping not need a building permit?

Simple like-for-like replacement of a few stumps on a minor Class 10 accessory structure can sometimes sit under exemptions. A licensed building surveyor decides that. Do not assume a house restump, a change of stump type, or re-levelling a dwelling is exempt.

Who issues a restumping building permit in Victoria?

A registered building surveyor. You can use a municipal building surveyor through your local council, or a private registered building surveyor. The VBA does not typically issue the permit.

What documents are typically needed for a restumping permit?

Surveyors commonly ask for a soil report or site classification, engineer drawings for the new stumps, and existing-conditions information such as an under-floor inspection or stump plan. Confirm the list with your surveyor.

Does the VBA issue restumping permits?

No. Permits are issued by a registered building surveyor (council or private). The VBA oversees practitioners and owner-builder requirements. “VBA restumping” searches are usually pointing at the wrong office.

How much does restumping cost in Victoria?

Installed restumping typically costs $500–$1,000+ per stump. A full restump is $20,000–$50,000; a partial restump of 10–20 stumps is $5,000–$15,000. Those figures are supply-and-install bands from the restumping cost guide, not building-permit application fees.
